> Small charities already have what they need for funding bids and posts. It sits in minutes, spreadsheets and old newsletters. I built demo organisations and sessions that show how to turn it into content that still sounds like them.

## [THE PROBLEM] Good material, no time

Small charities hold useful material in meeting minutes, survey spreadsheets, budgets and past posts. Turning it into bids, reports and public content takes time, and generic AI output loses the organisation's voice.

## [MY PART] Designed and delivered

I designed and delivered two sessions: a grants workshop and an AI marketing session. For each one I built a realistic demo organisation, so the room could watch the whole process on material like their own.

## [HOW IT WORKS] A charity that doesn't exist

**Kingsmead Community Trust** is fictional. It has brand notes, steering-group minutes, survey results, quarterly impact figures and a set of image assets.

- One line in the minutes becomes a social post and a flyer.
- Survey numbers become a stat graphic.
- A scheduled task drafts a weekly post, and an agent sets up a draft event listing.
- A writing-voice skill, built from a real local charity's public posts, is shown next to a generic AI draft of the same brief.

[[image:km-timetable]]

For the grants workshop I built two more demo organisations. Each has an organisation bible, a constitution, a budget and survey data, plus a grant application and a progress report. A grants prompt guide goes with them.

## [DESIGN DECISION] Match the room

>> Nobody should have to translate the example back to their own charity.

Kingsmead runs a food pantry, a youth club, a digital drop-in and a befriending scheme with a small volunteer team. That is the world the people in the room work in.
