> Putting on her favourite show took two remotes and a lot of menus. Now my grandmother taps a card.

## [THE PROBLEM] Two remotes, too many menus

Getting to her show or the news meant juggling the TV remote, the Sky remote and several menus. I wanted it to take one tap.

## [MY PART] Designed, directed, tested

I designed the app and directed the build. Then I tested it on the real TV and Sky box, and installed it on her phone.

[[image:gt-mode]]

## [HOW IT WORKS] Cards, not menus

- **NFC cards.** One plays her show on YouTube. Another switches to the Sky box and goes to the news channel.
- **Grandma mode.** The app opens with big buttons only. Setup is hidden behind a long press and hides itself again on restart.
- **Feedback she can feel.** A green message and two short buzzes when it works. A red message and a longer buzz when it doesn't.
- **A playlist that updates itself.** Her videos cycle without repeating, and I can change the list from a plain text file online. A cached copy covers a dropped connection, so new videos never need a reinstall.
- A normal remote screen for everything else.
- Everything runs over the home network. There's no cloud account.

[[image:ph-cards]]

## [DESIGN DECISION] Hide the settings

>> What she sees is only what she needs.

The settings are still there, but she can't reach them by accident.

## [EVIDENCE] Built in versions, checked on the real TV

- Four versions, 0.1 to 0.4.
- Pairing, playback, volume and switching to the Sky box were checked on the physical TV.
- I got it onto her phone with a LimeWire link and a QR code, both new to me.

[[image:gt-install]]

## [STAGE] Installed, used sometimes

It's on her phone and she uses it sometimes. It isn't part of her routine yet.
